00 — Brand Strategy
00 · Brand Strategy

A contemporary cultural ecosystem of the Vedic tradition.

ved·Life translates a vast body of timeless knowledge into objects and experiences built for contemporary life — made with the rigor of a modern studio and the depth of an ancient source. A cultural brand first; publishing is one expression of it, not its ceiling.

Every product carries the authority of an ancient source and the clarity of the present moment. The brand’s entire job is to hold these in balance — removing everything between a person and the wisdom itself: no mystification, no esoteric clutter, no gatekeeping. We honour the depth of the source; what we reject is the costume draped over it.

Operating principle
Preservation Through Transformation
Continuity survives only because the knowledge keeps finding new forms — transmitted, reinterpreted, redesigned through new media while remaining fundamentally itself.
Positioning

Treats this knowledge as living culture — rigorously sourced, clearly told, designed to the standard of the world’s best contemporary studios. Reads as culture, never religion.

The name

ved·Life = Veda + Life. It states the whole thesis in one word: ancient knowledge, lived now.

Voice

Wise & authoritative, minimal & refined, calm & serene. The confidence of an expert, not the volume of a guru. Confident, never loud.

VOICE
00b — Tone of Voice
00b · Tone of Voice

An editor,
not a guru.

The voice set in 00, spelled out. Words follow the mark: the source crosses the threshold word for word, the frame around it is made new. ved·Life writes the way a good editor speaks about a text they love — precise, unhurried, never selling transcendence.

Wise & authoritative
Know the source.

Name the text, the verse, the author, the translator, the century. Specifics instead of atmosphere — the confidence of an expert, not the volume of a guru.

Minimal & refined
Fewer words, the exact one.

One thought per sentence. No haze of epithets — ancient, mystical, sacred. Address one reader, not an audience.

Calm & serene
Don’t hurry. Don’t promise.

No exclamations, no urgency, no promise to change a life. The knowledge changes its form; the reader comes on their own.

The source, word for word · the frame, ours
So
“You have a right to your work, but never to its fruits.”
Bhagavad-gītā 2.47 · ved·Life translation

The verse is named, the translation signed. Everything around it — type, layout, format — is of today.

Not so
The Gita says: let go, and the universe will reward you.

No source. A paraphrase has replaced the text and added a promise the verse never makes.

One voice, four places

The same book, from the title page to the poster. The register shifts; the voice does not.

Metaphor 01 · the threshold

A doorway is a change of state.

Darśana — seeing, and being seen — happens across a threshold: you pass through and something is transmitted, unchanged in essence yet newly present. The brand does the same: it carries a fixed inheritance through a doorway into now.

That crossing — the same thing, on the other side — is the whole idea. The mark is built to hold it.

Metaphor 02 · transmission
Hand to hand — a thing is only kept alive by being given on.
Metaphor 03 · re-formation
“Die to live.” Form is let go so the essence can continue.
Made a shape

One idea, drawn.

A fixed square — the written text, the structure that must not be lost — turned through every stage of its own becoming, resolving to a circle: the living centre. The mark is the crossing itself, not either side of it.

01c — Exploration & Process
01c · Exploration & Process

The routes
not taken.

Before the mark could mean transformation, five honest directions were tried and set aside. Each failed a specific test — and each rejection sharpened the brief.

Route A
Concentric unity
Calm to the point of inert. Unity — but no tension between states, nothing becoming.
Route B
The merger
Square-meets-circle as a Venn reads like a tech merger. It states duality, not transformation.
व
Route C
The glyph
A Devanagari letter gates the brand behind one script and leans mystical. We remove barriers, not raise them.
Route D
The open book
Too literal — and it chains us to publishing. ved·Life is a culture, not only a press.
Route E
Rotation study
Rotation implies passage. But three frames read as a logo mid-glitch — it needed to resolve.
six tried
five set aside
one resolved
Chosen · the re-formed unit
Every state of one form over a single centre, resolving to a core. The mark is the transition — not either side of it. It works in one colour, at one centre, and it means exactly what the brand means: Preservation Through Transformation.

BISTER
08b · The Accent

The accent itself means preservation.

The rule holds: the mark must work in one colour before any colour is introduced. The lead accent is Bister. It anchors a wider pigment palette that follows overleaf.

On the mark, colour enters only through the resolved core — the endpoint of the transformation. The concentric rings and the wordmark always stay ink or paper.

Why bister
Bister is the warm sepia of iron-gall ink — the very ink manuscripts were copied and kept in for centuries. The accent itself means preservation.

SANSKRIT
11b — Sanskrit & Transliteration
11b · Sanskrit & Transliteration

Set the
words correctly.

Three registers, one rule from the threshold: the name of a work crosses unchanged, every diacritic intact; the names we give — series, events, products — are ours, and set plainly. Diacritics are part of the word, not an ornament: if a font can’t set them, the font is wrong.

Register 01 · Scholarly
Bhagavad-gītā

Full IAST. The name of a work, a verse, an author — wherever a reader meets it: title page, colophon, quote credit, shop, post.

Register 02 · Reading
Bhagavad Gita

Diacritics dropped. The names that are ours — The Upanishads series, Kirtan Nights — and any field where a reader types the word.

Register 03 · Technical
bhagavad-gita

ASCII only, hyphenated, lowercase. URLs, file names, metadata, search keys. Never shown to a reader as a title.

GROUND
12c — Ground & Light
12c · Ground & Light

The page
is part of it.

Every surface stands on a ground and is lit from one corner, like a page read by a lamp. The light gives depth and warmth; it never carries meaning. Paper, light, then at most one pattern — built in that order, the page is recognisable before a single mark appears.

How the light falls.

  • One source of light per surface — never two on paper.
  • Light comes from a corner: top-right on paper; warm bottom-right and cool top-left on ink.
  • It fades to nothing before 60 % of the width and never crosses the text block.
  • The pattern sits under the light, not over it.
  • Text holds 4.5 : 1 at the brightest point of the light — check it there.

What it never does.

  • Light on the mark, on type, on rules or on text cards — the mark stays flat.
  • Coloured light on paper. Paper takes only ember.
  • Rainbow or two-hue blends; light as decoration.
  • Light standing in for meaning — it never codes a part, a series or a state.
  • Light on merch and in single-colour print.

Surface by surface.

On photographs, a scrim — not a light. Warm ink #140E08 from the side the text sits on, 78–92 % at the text, just enough for 4.5 : 1. It is there to read, not to set a mood.

In print and on screen. Light is printed as a process tint under the ground; on uncoated stock or in one colour, drop it and keep the ground. On screen, use the values as written — they are CSS. On a portrait format the light keeps its corner; the ellipse turns with the page.

15 — Glyphs & UI Icons
15 · Glyphs & UI Icons

Marks,
not pictures.

ved·Life draws no pictograms of ideas — a temple, a flame, a scroll would undo everything the identity avoids. Instead: eight territory glyphs cut from the Continuum’s own arc unit, and a plain licensed set for interface work. One circle, eight cuts: every glyph is cut from the same circle on the same grid — the circle the mark resolves into. No cut is fuller than another; together they make the circle whole again.

Construction

A 48pt square of four 24pt cells. Three permitted elements only: the quarter-arc of radius ½ cell — the Continuum tile’s unit — a straight cell edge, and a filled dot. Stroke 3.2pt, butt caps.

One circle, eight cuts. At the centre of the grid sits one circle of radius 12 — the circle the mark resolves into: the essence. Each territory receives its own cut of it, on the same grid, at the same radius. No cut is fuller or truer than another; a glyph means its territory, as a letter does. Together the eight make the circle whole again — Preservation Through Transformation, one essence in many forms.

How to read a cut. Every element keeps one meaning in every glyph. The dot is the source — the bindu of the yantra the mark comes from. The circle is transmission; how much of it is drawn says how a territory carries it, never how well. The straight line is the axis — the way down from above. An arc beyond the circle is transmission crossing its frame.

Colour, as in the mark: only the centre carries pigment — the dot, in the pigment of its claim (13 · Thresholds): indigo received, terracotta retold, marigold happening now. On a coloured ground the dot turns gold #F0C98A. A glyph without a dot stays one colour: its source is not drawn.
